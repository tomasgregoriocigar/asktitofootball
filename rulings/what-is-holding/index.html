You Asked

What is holding?

Tito Interpretation

Holding comes in two versions, and they are not priced the same. Offensive holding — a blocker using his hands or arms to materially restrict a defender — costs 10 yards. Defensive holding — a defender grasping a receiver or cutting off his path — costs only 5 yards, but it comes with an automatic first down. The asymmetry is deliberate: the defensive version can kill a play before it starts, so the book refunds the down along with the yards.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Everyone sees the jersey grab; the book asks a different question — did the blocker materially restrict the defender’s path or angle of pursuit? Hands inside the frame can still be holding, and a grab that changes nothing is not. On the defensive side, fans expect 10 yards like the offense gets; the book says 5 yards and a fresh set of downs, which is why a defender will take the flag over giving up the completion.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 12, Section 1, Article 3 makes it a foul for an offensive blocker to use his hands or arms to materially restrict or alter a defender’s path or angle of pursuit — grabbing or tackling an opponent, hooking, jerking, twisting, or pulling him to the ground included — and sets the penalty for holding by the offense at a loss of 10 yards. Rule 8, Section 4, Article 6 defines defensive holding as grasping an eligible offensive player (or his jersey), or extending an arm or arms to cut off or encircle him, with a penalty of five yards and an automatic first down.
