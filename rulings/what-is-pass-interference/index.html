You Asked

What is pass interference?

Tito Interpretation

Pass interference is an act by any player, more than one yard beyond the line of scrimmage, that significantly hinders an eligible player's opportunity to catch a forward pass. Defensive interference runs from the moment the ball is thrown until it is touched: playing through a receiver's back, grabbing his arm, or cutting off his path without playing the ball are all fouls. The penalty is the harshest defensive foul in the book: the offense gets the ball at the spot of the foul with an automatic first down, even if the foul happened 40 yards downfield. If the interference happens in the end zone, the ball is placed at the 1-yard line, or half the distance to the goal if the offense was already inside the 2. Offensive pass interference costs 10 yards from the previous spot.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Fans call any contact interference. The book asks two questions first: was the act more than a yard downfield, and did it significantly hinder the catch? Incidental contact when both players are competing for the ball is not a foul, and neither is contact on a pass that is clearly uncatchable by the players involved. Fans also expect a flat 15 yards, like most big fouls. It is a spot foul, which is why one flag can be worth 40 yards.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 8, Section 5, Article 1 defines pass interference as an act by a player more than one yard beyond the line of scrimmage that significantly hinders an eligible player's opportunity to catch the ball. Article 4 sets the penalty for pass interference by the defense as a first down for the offensive team at the spot of the foul, and for pass interference by the offense as a loss of 10 yards from the previous spot.
