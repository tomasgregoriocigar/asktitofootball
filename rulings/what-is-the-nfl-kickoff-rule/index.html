You Asked

What is the NFL kickoff rule?

Tito Interpretation

On a kickoff, the kicker lines up at his own 35-yard line, and the other 10 kicking team players line up at the receiving team's 40-yard line. The receiving team lines up on or behind its 35-yard line, with at least nine players in the setup zone between its 35 and 30. Kicking team players (other than the kicker) and setup zone players cannot move until the ball touches the ground or a player in the landing zone (the receiving team's 20-yard line to the goal line) or the end zone. A kick that touches the ground or a player in the landing zone and is downed in the end zone, or goes out of bounds behind the goal line, is a touchback at the 20-yard line. A kick that goes into the end zone or out of bounds there without first touching the ground or a player in the landing zone is a touchback at the 35. A kick that first touches the ground or a player short of the landing zone is dead. Illegal formation or moving early costs five yards.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Fans remember the old kickoff, with both teams running full speed from 35 yards apart. The dynamic kickoff has a static alignment and a landing zone, and the touchback spot now depends on where the ball first lands (the 20 or the 35), which looks unfamiliar.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 6, Section 1, Article 5 provides that a kickoff or safety kick into the end zone that remains inbounds is a live ball and must be returned or downed by the receiving team. It is a touchback, with the dead ball spot at the 20-yard line, if the kick touches the ground or a player in the landing zone and is downed in the end zone or goes out of bounds behind the goal line, and a touchback with the dead ball spot at the 35-yard line if, without first touching the ground or a player in the landing zone, the kick goes out of bounds behind the goal line, strikes the goal post, uprights, or crossbar, or lands at or beyond the goal line and is downed in the end zone.
