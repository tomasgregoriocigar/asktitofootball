You Asked

What is the difference between defensive holding, illegal contact, and pass interference?

Tito Interpretation

The three fouls are divided by timing and by what the defender's hands do. Defensive holding is grabbing: grasping an eligible receiver or his jersey with the hands, or extending an arm to cut off or encircle him. Illegal contact is initiating contact with a receiver while the quarterback still has the ball in the pocket and has not thrown it; within five yards of the line a defender may chuck a receiver in front of him, but he cannot initiate contact beyond that zone, and he cannot let go of a receiver inside the zone and hit him a second time. Pass interference only starts once the ball is in the air, and it requires hindering the catch itself, more than a yard downfield. Holding and illegal contact each cost 5 yards and an automatic first down. Interference is a spot foul and a first down.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

The flags look identical on television, so fans treat them as one foul with three names. The book reads the play in windows: before the throw, hands on a receiver are holding or illegal contact and cost 5 yards; after the throw, contact that hinders the catch is interference, and the price jumps to the spot of the foul.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 8, Section 4, Article 1 allows a defender to chuck an eligible receiver within five yards of the line of scrimmage; Articles 2 and 3 define illegal contact within and beyond that zone; and Article 6 defines defensive holding, with a penalty of a loss of five yards and an automatic first down, the same penalty as illegal contact. Rule 8, Section 5, Article 1 defines pass interference, which can only occur when a forward pass is thrown.

NFL Rulebook — Rule 8, Section 4, Articles 1, 2, 3, and 6; Rule 8, Section 5, Article 1
