You Asked

I thought running out of bounds stops the clock.

Tito Interpretation

It does, briefly. Outside the last 2 minutes of the first half and the last 5 minutes of the game, the clock restarts on the referee's ready-for-play signal once the ball is set. Inside those windows it stays stopped until the snap. And if the runner's forward progress was stopped in bounds, the clock never stops at all.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

The stop is real but momentary; fans see the stop, not the quiet restart.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 4, Section 3, Article 2 provides that when a runner goes out of bounds on a play from scrimmage, the game clock starts on the Referee’s signal that a ball has been returned to play, except that the clock will start on the snap after a change of possession, after the two-minute warning of the first half, or inside the last five minutes of the second half.
