You Asked

Did Parker Washington make the catch for a touchdown against the Giants?

Tito Interpretation

By the book's own words, it was a catch: once Washington secured control with his feet inbounds, the ball moving or switching hands in his grasp on the way to the ground is not a loss of control, and he never lost it. It stayed incomplete for a different reason: the ruling on the field was incomplete, and replay can only change a ruling on clear and obvious evidence.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Fans argue about whether the ball moved. The book settled that argument in advance: movement is not loss of control, and a receiver only fails to survive the ground if he actually loses control and the ball hits the ground before he regains it. Washington was told no because the ball switched hands. Switching hands while keeping a grasp is movement, and movement alone does not break a catch.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 8, Section 1, Article 3 makes a forward pass complete if a player who is inbounds secures control of the ball prior to the ball touching the ground, touches the ground inbounds with both feet or with any part of his body other than his hands, and, after those two steps have been fulfilled, clearly performs any act common to the game, or maintains control of the ball long enough to do so. Its notes add that movement of the ball does not automatically result in loss of control, and that a player does not survive the ground only if, before completing the catch, he contacts the ground and loses control of the ball and the ball hits the ground before he regains control. Separately, Rule 15, Section 2, Article 1 provides that an on-field ruling will be changed only when clear and obvious video evidence warrants a change, which is the standard the review applied when the incomplete ruling stood.
