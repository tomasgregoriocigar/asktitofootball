You Asked

What is a sack?

Tito Interpretation

A sack is the defense tackling the quarterback behind the line of scrimmage while he still has the ball and is trying to pass. There is no flag and no foul — the play simply ends where he goes down, the offense loses those yards, and the down counts. If the quarterback gets the throw away first, it is a pass play, not a sack.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Fans hunt for the word in the rulebook and never find it, because “sack” is a statistic, not a ruling — the word does not appear in the book at all. What the book rules is the tackle itself: a runner contacted by an opponent who touches the ground with anything but his hands or feet is down, and the down is over. The sack is just that rule applied to the one runner everyone is watching.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 7, Section 2, Article 1 has the official declare the ball dead and the down ended when a runner is contacted by an opponent and touches the ground with any part of his body other than his hands (including the wrist) or feet, or when a runner is held or otherwise restrained so that his forward progress ends. A quarterback tackled behind the line before releasing a pass is exactly that runner — the book ends the down at the spot, and the term “sack” lives in the statistics, not the rules.
