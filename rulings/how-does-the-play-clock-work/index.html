You Asked

How does the play clock work?

Tito Interpretation

The play clock is 40 seconds, starting the moment the previous play ends. After an administrative stoppage (a timeout, a change of possession, a penalty enforcement, the two-minute warning, the end of a period, a Try, or a free kick) it is 25 seconds, starting on the referee's whistle. Let it reach zero before the snap and it is delay of game, 5 yards.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Fans see 25 after every timeout and think the clock shrank. It did not shrink. The 40 only runs in the normal flow of play; any stoppage the officials have to administer restarts the offense on the shorter 25.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 4, Section 6, Article 1 sets a 40-second play clock, timed from when a play ends, and makes it delay of game if the ball is not put in play within that interval. Article 2 sets a 25-second play clock, beginning with the Referee’s whistle, after administrative stoppages such as a change of possession, a charged team timeout, the two-minute warning, the expiration of a period, a penalty enforcement, or a free kick.
