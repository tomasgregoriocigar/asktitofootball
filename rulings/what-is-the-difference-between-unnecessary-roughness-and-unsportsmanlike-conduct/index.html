You Asked

What is the difference between unnecessary roughness and unsportsmanlike conduct?

Tito Interpretation

Both cost 15 yards, but they police different things. Unnecessary roughness is physical: Rule 12, Section 2 collects the contact fouls, and Article 8 covers violence the play did not call for — forcibly contacting a runner who is out of bounds, running into a runner whose forward progress has been stopped, throwing a runner to the ground after the ball is dead. Unsportsmanlike conduct is behavior: Rule 12, Section 3 covers acts contrary to the principles of sportsmanship that need no contact at all — taunting, abusive or insulting language and gestures, violent gestures, prolonged celebrations. Roughness is judged by what a player did to an opponent; conduct is judged by what he did to the game. When the defense commits either foul, it is also an automatic first down, and either can bring a disqualification when the officials judge the action flagrant.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Fans treat the two flags as synonyms because the yardage is identical. Watch the act instead of the number: a hit, a throw-down, a pile-on is roughness; a mouth, a gesture, a celebration is conduct, and several conduct fouls can be penalized anywhere in the stadium, before, during, or after the game. The split is the book's own: Section 2 measures the act against the play, Section 3 measures it against sportsmanship itself.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 12, Section 2, Article 8 provides that there shall be no unnecessary roughness, including forcibly contacting a runner when he is out of bounds and running, diving into, or throwing the body against or on a runner whose forward progress has been stopped, with a penalty of 15 yards, an automatic first down if the foul is by the defense, and disqualification if the action is judged flagrant. Rule 12, Section 3, Article 1 provides that there shall be no unsportsmanlike conduct — any act which is contrary to the generally understood principles of sportsmanship, specifically including taunting, abusive, threatening, or insulting language or gestures, violent gestures, and prolonged or excessive celebrations or demonstrations — with a penalty of 15 yards, an automatic first down if the foul is by the defense, and disqualification if the action is flagrant.

NFL Rulebook — Rule 12, Section 2, Article 8; Rule 12, Section 3, Article 1
