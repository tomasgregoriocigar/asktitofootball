You Asked

What can be challenged in the NFL?

Tito Interpretation

Only the plays on Rule 15, Section 3's list. A coach can challenge whether a pass was complete, whether a player was down by contact, fumbles and recoveries, plays governed by the goal line, the boundary lines, the line of scrimmage and the line to gain, whether a pass was forward or backward or illegally thrown, the number of players on the field, the status of the game clock, and the disqualification of a player. Each team gets a minimum of two challenges, each one requires an available timeout, a failed challenge costs the timeout, and a team successful on at least one challenge earns a third. Judgment fouls are not on the list at all — pass interference and holding can never be challenged.

Why Everyone Gets It Wrong

Fans treat the red flag as a general appeal: if the call was wrong, throw it. The book makes it a closed list, not an appeal. And some plays never need a flag: after the two-minute warning of each half, on scoring plays, on Try attempts, and throughout overtime, only the Replay Official can start a review. The booth can also assist the crew on a short list of objective points under Rule 15, Section 9 — picking up a facemask or horse-collar foul when the video clearly shows no contact, for example — but that is the league's tool, not a coach's challenge.

Rule Book Confirmed

Rule 15, Section 1, Article 1 permits each team a minimum of two challenges, each requiring an available team timeout, with a third challenge permitted if the team is successful on at least one of its challenges, and limits the head coach to the on-field rulings listed in Section 3. Section 3 covers plays involving possession, touching of the ball or the ground, the goal line, the boundary lines, the line of scrimmage, the line to gain, the number of players on the field, game administration including the status of the game clock, disqualification of a player, and other listed reviewable plays. Rule 15, Section 4 lists aspects of plays that are not reviewable, including whether a passer intentionally grounded a pass and whether a receiver was illegally contacted, and provides that any aspect of a play not listed as reviewable in Section 3 is not reviewable.
